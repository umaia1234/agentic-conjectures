TxGraffiti Conjecture 3 (i(G) <= mu*(G) for r-regular G): search summary
=========================================================================

Everything below is computer-assisted and local only (not run in CI, not formalized).
`sh reproduce.sh STAGE` reruns each block; the stage names are given in brackets.
Timings are wall-clock seconds on one core of an Apple-silicon Mac (October 2026).

Checker: tx3check.c.  For each graph6 graph it computes i(G) exactly by branch and bound.  If
i < LB = ceil(m/(2r-1)) then i < LB <= mu*, so the graph is not a counterexample and nothing more
is computed.  Otherwise it computes mu* exactly (the least k >= LB with a maximal matching of size
<= k).  Columns: graphs = graphs read; needmu = graphs with i >= LB (mu* computed); tight = graphs
with i = mu*; cex = graphs with i > mu*.
Generation: nauty geng -c -dR -DR n (connected R-regular graphs, one per isomorphism class).

Checker validation [xcheck]
---------------------------
* tx3check -v (exact i and mu* for every graph) agrees with the pysat solvers of tx3lib.py
  (xcheck_c.py) on all 18,058 connected r-regular graphs with r = 3..6 and 6 <= n <= 12.
* crosscheck.py 2000: random r-regular graphs (r = 3..6, n <= 14); i by pysat, CP-SAT and
  enumeration of maximal independent sets, mu* by pysat and CP-SAT (and brute force on every
  fifth graph with at most 30 edges); witnesses checked.  No disagreement (about 10 s).
* G48 [g48]: tx3check prints COUNTEREXAMPLE i=16 mu*=15 (also a CI check in status.yaml).

Total perfect codes (not in reproduce.sh; recorded for completeness)
  A total perfect code W of a cubic graph (every vertex has exactly one neighbour in W) gives
  two weighted blow-ups: t = 2 on W and 1 elsewhere is 4-regular, t = 1 on W and 2 elsewhere is
  5-regular.  None of the 114 cubic counterexamples we had (G48, and the 41 graphs on 50 and 72 on
  60 vertices of Iwane's collection) has a total perfect code: pysat finds none; for the 50-vertex
  graphs this is forced by 3 not dividing 50.

Unsatisfiable balanced CNF formulas [sa]  (sa_balanced_cnf.c)
  For an r-regular graph with an efficient edge dominating set (an induced matching M that
  dominates every edge), the M-edges are complementary literal pairs and the other vertices are
  clauses of an r-CNF in which every literal occurs exactly r - 1 times, and mu* = |M|.  If the
  formula is satisfiable then the true literals form an IDS of size |M|, so i <= mu*.  A
  counterexample of this kind therefore needs an unsatisfiable (r-1)-balanced r-CNF (DETAILS.md).
  Simulated annealing over such formulas (exact #SAT over all 2^v assignments, temperature on
  log(#SAT + 1)), seeds as in reproduce.sh:
    k = 3, v = 15, c = 20:  T = (0.3, 0.01): unsatisfiable formula at restart 1 (19 s);
                            T = (0.1, 0.005): best #SAT 4;  T = (0.05, 0.002): unsatisfiable
                            formula at restart 2.  Both formulas were re-checked with CaDiCaL and
                            by counting (0 satisfying assignments, every literal exactly twice).
    k = 4 (each literal exactly 3 times), 4 restarts of 2,000,000 steps, T = (0.3, 0.01):
      v = 12, c = 18:  best #SAT 424 of 4,096
      v = 14, c = 21:  best #SAT 1,183 of 16,384
      v = 16, c = 24:  best #SAT 3,516 of 65,536
      v = 18, c = 27:  best #SAT 10,356 of 262,144
      v = 20, c = 30:  best #SAT 30,860 of 1,048,576
  The k = 4 runs come nowhere near an unsatisfiable formula.  Whether an unsatisfiable
  (4,3,3)-formula exists is open (Zhang-Peitl-Szeider, arXiv:2405.16149; Iwane, Problem 17).
